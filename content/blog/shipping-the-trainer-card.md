most portfolios open on a hero and a wall of projects. mine opens on a pixel trainer card.

that wasn't a gimmick first. it was a constraint: if the first screen has to feel like *something*, what does "something" look like for a builder who grew up on gba games and ships at weird hours?

## the bet

a personal site is a product. products need a first impression that sticks. the card does three jobs at once:

1. **identity** — name, role, the vibe, before you read a single paragraph
2. **navigation** — flip it, dig into projects, skills, the journey
3. **memory** — people remember the pixel thing longer than another dark landing page

## what i learned shipping it

- motion has to earn its keep. the flip and the flash transition exist so the two worlds (card ↔ portfolio) feel connected, not like two random pages.
- content still matters more than chrome. the card is the hook; the `/portfolio` pages are where the work lives.
- ship the weird version. polish comes after people can click it.

## what's next

a blog (you're reading it), then a little partner in the corner that actually knows my work. same rule as always: build the thing, put it live, iterate in public.
